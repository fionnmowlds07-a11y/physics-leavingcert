Part 1 — Motion

🏎️ Speed, Velocity & Displacement

The foundation of mechanics: understanding how things move from A to B.

Distance vs Displacement
Distance is a scalar (magnitude only) e.g., 50m.
Displacement is a vector (magnitude + direction) e.g., 50m North. It is the straight-line distance from start to finish.
Speed vs Velocity
Speed = Distance / Time (scalar).
Velocity = Displacement / Time (vector). Rate of change of displacement.

Measuring Average Speed (Ticker Tape)

A ticker-tape timer makes 50 dots per second on a strip of paper. The time interval between any two consecutive dots is exactly 0.02 seconds (1/50). If a car pulls the tape, you measure the distance of 5 spaces (0.1s) to calculate average velocity: v = s / t.

Part 1 — Motion

📈 Acceleration & Graphs

What happens when velocity isn't constant.

📖 Definition — Acceleration
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time. Unit: m/s².
⚠️ The Equations of Motion (Learn these!)
1. v = u + at
2. s = ut + ½at²
3. v² = u² + 2as
u = Initial velocity (starts from rest = 0)
v = Final velocity (comes to a stop = 0)
a = Acceleration
s = Displacement
t = Time

Velocity-Time Graphs

  • A horizontal line means constant velocity (zero acceleration).
  • A straight sloped line means constant acceleration.
  • Crucial Exam Rule: The slope of the graph equals the Acceleration. The Area under the curve equals the Total Distance Travelled!
Part 1 — Motion

🍎 Gravity & Experiments

Falling objects and measuring 'g'.

Acceleration due to gravity on Earth is represented by g (approx 9.8 m/s²). All objects fall at this same rate in a vacuum, regardless of their mass (feather and hammer fall together without air resistance).

⚠️ Exam Note: When an object is thrown upwards, gravity slows it down, so use a = -9.8 m/s². At its maximum height, its velocity is exactly 0 m/s.

Mandatory Experiment: To Measure 'g'

Apparatus: Electromagnet, steel ball, trapdoor, electronic timer, metre stick.

  1. Measure distance (s) from bottom of ball to trapdoor.
  2. Flick switch to release ball and start timer.
  3. Ball hits trapdoor, stopping timer. Record time (t).
  4. Repeat for different heights. Plot a graph of s against t².
  5. The slope of this graph is ½g. Therefore, g = 2 × slope.
Part 2 — Forces

💥 Newton's Laws

The three fundamental rules of the universe.

📖 Definition — Force
A force is anything that causes an object to accelerate (change its velocity).

Newton's Three Laws of Motion

Law 1 (Inertia)
An object will remain at rest or travelling at a constant velocity unless an external force acts on it. (e.g., wearing a seatbelt prevents you continuing forward when a car crashes).
Law 2 (F=ma)
The rate of change of momentum is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction of the force. F = ma.
Law 3 (Action/Reaction)
If body A exerts a force on body B, body B exerts an equal but opposite force on body A. (e.g., a rocket pushes gas down, gas pushes rocket up).

Mass vs Weight

Mass is the amount of matter in an object (kg). It never changes. Weight is the force of gravity acting on a mass (Newtons). It changes depending on what planet you are on! Formula: W = mg.

Part 2 — Forces

🎳 Momentum

The "unstoppability" of moving objects.

⚠️ Formula — Momentum
p = mv
p = Momentum (kg m/s)
m = Mass (kg)
v = Velocity (m/s)
📖 Definition — Principle of Conservation of Momentum
In any closed system (no external forces), the total momentum before an interaction is equal to the total momentum after the interaction.

This principle is used to solve all collision and explosion problems.
Formula: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

If two objects stick together after a crash, combine their masses: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v

Friction

Friction is a force that opposes motion. It's needed to walk or drive, but reduces efficiency in engines. It can be reduced using lubrication or ball bearings.

Part 3 — Energy

⚡ Work & Energy

The ability to do things in the physical world.

Work
Done when a force moves an object.
W = Fs (Force × displacement). Unit: Joules (J).
Energy
The ability to do work. Unit: Joules (J).
📖 Definition — Conservation of Energy
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, but only converted from one form to another.
⚠️ Formulas — Kinetic & Potential Energy
E_k = ½mv²
E_p = mgh
E_k = Kinetic Energy (Energy due to motion).
E_p = Potential Energy (Energy due to position/height).

Example: A falling pendulum loses Potential Energy and gains Kinetic Energy, reaching maximum Kinetic Energy at the very bottom of its swing.

Part 3 — Energy

🔋 Power & Efficiency

How fast work gets done, and how much is wasted.

📖 Definition — Power
Power is the rate at which work is done (or the rate of energy conversion). Unit: Watts (W), where 1 Watt = 1 Joule per second.
P = W / t

Percentage Efficiency

No machine is 100% efficient. Some energy is always lost, usually as heat or sound due to friction.

% Efficiency = (Useful Energy Out / Total Energy In) × 100

The Solar Constant

The solar constant is the average amount of the Sun's energy falling per second perpendicularly on 1 metre squared of the Earth's atmosphere. It is approximately 1.35 kW/m².

Part 4 — Fluids & Gravity

🎈 Density & Pressure

The physics of fluids, floating, and squeezing gases.

Density (ρ)
Mass per unit volume.
ρ = m / V. Unit: kg/m³.
Pressure (P)
Force per unit area.
P = F / A. Unit: Pascals (Pa).
Pressure in a Liquid
Increases with depth.
P = ρgh.
📖 Definition — Boyle's Law
For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, its pressure is inversely proportional to its volume (P ∝ 1/V, or P₁V₁ = P₂V₂).
📖 Definition — Archimedes' Principle
When an object is wholly or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upthrust equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.
💡 Law of Flotation: The weight of a floating object is exactly equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces. A heavy steel ship floats because its hollow shape displaces a massive volume of water! A hydrometer is an instrument based on this principle used to measure liquid density.
Part 4 — Fluids & Gravity

🌍 Universal Gravity

The force pulling planets, stars, and people together.

📖 Definition — Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation
Any two point masses in the universe attract each other with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses, and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
⚠️ Formula — Law of Gravitation
F = G(m₁m₂) / d²
F = Gravitational Force (N)
G = Gravitational Constant (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹)
m₁, m₂ = The masses of the two objects (kg)
d = The distance between their centers (m)

Weight and Gravity Connection

Since Weight (W) = mg, and Weight is just the gravitational force F, we can say: mg = G(Mm)/d². The small 'm' cancels out, giving us the formula to calculate 'g' on any planet!

g = GM / d²
Part 4 — Fluids & Gravity

⚖️ Moments & Couples

Levers, see-saws, and the turning effect of forces.

📖 Definition — Moment of a Force
The moment of a force is the turning effect of a force. It is calculated as the Force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the fulcrum (pivot).
M = F × d

Conditions for Equilibrium

For an object (like a metre stick) to be completely balanced and not moving or rotating:

  1. The sum of the upward forces must equal the sum of the downward forces.
  2. The sum of the clockwise moments about any point must equal the sum of the anticlockwise moments.
A Couple
Two parallel forces with the same magnitude acting in opposite directions. Resultant force is zero, but they create a pure turning effect. (e.g., two hands turning a steering wheel). Torque = Force × distance between them.
Part 5 — Advanced Mechanics

➡️ Vectors & Inclined Planes

Breaking angled forces into vertical and horizontal chunks.

A vector has both magnitude and direction. If two forces pull on an object at an angle, you can find the Resultant Force using the Parallelogram Law or Triangle Law.

Resolving Vectors

More often, we have one angled force and need to break it into its horizontal (X) and vertical (Y) parts.

⚠️ Formula — Resolving Vectors
X = F cos(θ)
Y = F sin(θ)
X = The component adjacent to the angle.
Y = The component opposite the angle.
F = The main angled force vector.

The Inclined Plane

When a block is on a ramp at angle θ, gravity (mg) pulls straight down. We resolve gravity into two parts relative to the ramp:

  • Parallel to ramp: mg sin(θ). This is the force pulling the block DOWN the ramp.
  • Perpendicular to ramp: mg cos(θ). This is the force pulling the block INTO the ramp.
Part 5 — Advanced Mechanics

🎡 Circular Motion

What happens when things don't go in a straight line.

An object moving in a circle at a constant speed is always accelerating because its direction is constantly changing. This requires a force pulling it inwards.

📖 Definition — Centripetal Force
The force directed towards the center of a circle that is required to keep an object moving in a circular path.

Angular Velocity (ω)

Measured in radians per second (rad/s). The rate of change of angle.

⚠️ Formulas — Circular Motion
ω = θ / t
v = rω
a = rω² = v²/r
F = mrω² = mv²/r
ω (omega) = Angular velocity (rad/s)
v = Linear speed (m/s)
r = Radius (m)
F = Centripetal Force (N)

Satellites & Orbits

A satellite orbiting Earth is kept in circular motion entirely by Earth's gravity. Therefore, Centripetal Force = Gravitational Force.
mv²/r = GMm/r².
This leads to the derivation for the period of an orbit: T² = (4π²r³) / GM.

A Geostationary Satellite is one that orbits exactly above the equator with a period of 24 hours, so it appears to stay stationary in the sky. Used for communications and weather.

Part 5 — Advanced Mechanics

⏱️ Simple Harmonic Motion

The physics of springs, bouncing, and swinging pendulums.

📖 Definition — Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)
An object moves with SHM if its acceleration is directly proportional to its distance from a fixed point, and is always directed towards that point.
⚠️ Formulas — SHM & Hooke's Law
a = -ω²s
F = -ks
T = 2π / ω
a = Acceleration
s = Displacement (distance from rest position)
ω² = Constant. The minus sign shows 'a' and 's' are opposite directions.
F = -ks is Hooke's Law (k = spring constant).
T = Periodic Time (time for one full oscillation).

The Simple Pendulum

A mass swinging on a string undergoes SHM provided the angle of swing is small (less than 5°). The periodic time of a pendulum is given by: T = 2π√(l/g), where l is the length of the pendulum.

Exam Prep

📐 Calculations

Step-by-step examples of typical Leaving Cert questions.

🧮 Example 1 — Equations of Motion
Q: A car traveling at 20 m/s accelerates steadily at 3 m/s² for 5 seconds. What is its final velocity, and how far did it travel?
Step 1 — Identify Variables
u = 20, a = 3, t = 5. We need 'v' and 's'.
Step 2 — Final Velocity (v)
v = u + at
v = 20 + (3)(5) = 35 m/s
Step 3 — Distance (s)
s = ut + ½at²
s = (20)(5) + ½(3)(5²)
s = 100 + 37.5 = 137.5 m
🧮 Example 2 — Conservation of Momentum
Q: A 1000 kg car traveling at 15 m/s crashes into a stationary 2000 kg truck. They stick together. What is their combined velocity after the crash?
Step 1 — Momentum Before
Car p = (1000)(15) = 15000.
Truck p = 0.
Total Before = 15000 kg m/s.
Step 2 — Momentum After
Combined Mass = 3000 kg.
Total After = 3000v.
Step 3 — Solve
15000 = 3000v
v = 5 m/s
🧮 Example 3 — Energy Conservation
Q: A 2 kg ball is dropped from a height of 10 m. What is its velocity right before it hits the ground? (g = 9.8)
Step 1 — Potential Energy at Top
Ep = mgh = (2)(9.8)(10) = 196 J.
Step 2 — Kinetic Energy at Bottom
All Ep converts to Ek. Ek = 196 J.
Step 3 — Solve for v
½mv² = 196
1(v²) = 196
v = √196 = 14 m/s
🧮 Example 4 — Centripetal Force
Q: A 0.5 kg stone attached to a 2 m string is spun in a circle at a linear speed of 4 m/s. Calculate the tension in the string.
Step 1 — Identify Variables
m = 0.5, r = 2, v = 4. Tension = Centripetal force.
Step 2 — Formula
F = mv² / r
F = (0.5)(4²) / 2 = 8 / 2 = 4 N.
🧮 Example 5 — Pressure & Density
Q: Calculate the pressure due to water at a depth of 20 m. (Density of water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step 1 — Identify Variables
h = 20, ρ = 1000, g = 9.8
Step 2 — Formula
P = ρgh
P = (1000)(9.8)(20) = 196,000 Pa.
