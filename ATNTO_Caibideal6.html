Draíocht an Cheoil

Maidhc's passion for the accordion, a letter to Cox of Kilcock, and the stunning revelation that his quiet father Dainín had learned to play music in Chicago — a chapter full of warmth and family surprise.

Bosca Ceoil / Accordion Cox of Kilcock Athair / Father Teaghlach / Family Chicago

📋 Background & Overview

Chapter 6 is one of the warmest chapters in the autobiography. Maidhc had a deep love of music — particularly the bosca ceoil (accordion). There was a weekly radio programme sponsored by Cox of Kilcock, a music shop that sold Hohner-made instruments. Maidhc and his brother Páidí wrote a letter asking for a catalogue, chose an accordion, and persuaded their mother to give them two pounds as a deposit.

The chapter's emotional peak is the arrival of the parcel at the post office — and the extraordinary revelation that Dainín, the usually reserved father, had learned to play music from Seán Coughlan when he lived in Chicago. Nobody in the family had known this.

🎯 Core Themes in this Chapter

  • Pastimes (Caitheamh Aimsire): Music was a central pastime — the accordion becomes Maidhc's great talent and a source of income in Chapter 8.
  • Family Life (Saol an Teaghlaigh): The parcel arrival brings the whole family together; Dainín's hidden talent is a moment of genuine family discovery and warmth.
  • A Different Side of Dainín: The revelation that Dainín learned music in Chicago shows he had a whole life before his family knew him — he becomes more rounded and human.
  • Community and Tradition: Music was the social glue of the Gaeltacht — céilí, dancing, communal evenings. Maidhc's love of music connects him to that tradition.
🎓
Exam Approach

Use Chapter 6 for questions about pastimes (music as a lifelong passion), family life (the parcel scene, Dainín's revelation), and a different/surprising side of characters. The Chicago revelation is particularly strong for showing the depth and complexity of family relationships in the book.

Key Scenes — Analysis + Irish Phrases to Use
Scene 1 — The Letter to Cox of Kilcock
Chuir Maidhc agus a dheartháir Páidí litir chucu ag iarraidh leabhar uirlisí a fháil uathu. Bhí an mháthair i ngiúmar maith agus thug sí dhá phunt dóibh mar chur síos.
Maidhc and his brother Páidí sent them a letter asking to get an instrument catalogue. The mother was in a good mood and gave them two pounds as a deposit.

Analysis (English)

This scene is charming in its detail — two boys writing a formal letter to a music shop, carefully choosing an instrument from a catalogue, and then working on their mother at the right moment to secure a deposit. The phrase "bhí an mháthair i ngiúmar maith" (the mother was in a good mood) is a delightful, knowing detail — Maidhc understood exactly how to time a request. It also shows the mother in a warm, generous light. The effort involved — the letter, the wait, the negotiation — makes the eventual arrival of the accordion all the more exciting.

Scene 2 — The Parcel Arrives
Tháinig beart bhreá mhór go dtí oifig an phoist Baile na nGall. Bhí gach duine sa teach bailithe timpeall.
A fine large parcel arrived at the Baile na nGall post office. Everyone in the house was gathered around.

Analysis (English)

This is a beautiful domestic scene — the whole family crowded around a parcel. In an era before online shopping or instant delivery, receiving a parcel by post was genuinely exciting. The image of everyone gathered around it captures the warmth of family life perfectly. In a house with no television, no computers, and very little money, the arrival of a new accordion was a major event. Maidhc paints this moment with real affection, and the reader can feel the excitement and anticipation in the room.

Scene 3 — Dainín's Secret: Music from Chicago
Sheinn athair Mhaidhc cúpla port ar an mbosca nua agus ní raibh fhios ag éinne sa chlann go raibh ceol aige. Dúirt sé gur fhoghlaim sé a chuid ceoil ó Seán Coughlan nuair a bhí sé i Chicago.
Maidhc's father played a few tunes on the new box and nobody in the family knew he had music. He said he had learned his music from Seán Coughlan when he was in Chicago.

Analysis (English)

This is the emotional centrepiece of the chapter and one of the most surprising and touching moments in the entire book. Dainín — the usually quiet, stern, serious father — picks up the new accordion and plays. Nobody knew he could play a note. This single moment completely reframes how the reader and family see him: here is a man who lived a whole life before his family, who emigrated to Chicago, who learned music there, and who carried that secret talent silently for years. It humanises Dainín in a profound way. It also suggests that the love of music Maidhc inherited came directly from his father — a hidden legacy finally revealed.

Scene 4 — Seán Coughlan's Visit and Maidhc Learns
Lá amháin tháinig Seán go dtí an teach agus sheinn sé ceol. Thug sé roinnt nodanna do Mhaidhc faoi sheinnt an bhosca. Chaith Maidhc a am saor á sheinnt.
One day Seán came to the house and played music. He gave Maidhc some tips on playing the accordion. Maidhc spent his free time playing it.

Analysis (English)

This scene completes the musical circle: Dainín learned from Seán Coughlan in Chicago, and now Seán comes to the house and passes that knowledge on to Maidhc. Music is shown as something that travels across generations and across the Atlantic, connecting the Gaeltacht community in Kerry to the Irish emigrant community in Chicago. Maidhc spending all his free time playing the accordion shows a genuine, deep passion — and it sets up the crucial role music will play in Chapter 8, when he plays for the céilís and meets Eibhlín.

Themes in Chapter 6

🎵 Music as Pastime

Music was not just entertainment — it was a central part of Gaeltacht life, tying people together at céilís, in houses, and across generations. Maidhc's passion for the accordion defines him and leads directly to employment and romance in Chapter 8.

👨‍👩‍👦 Family Life

The parcel scene is the warmest image of family life in the book — everyone gathered around in excitement. Dainín's secret musical talent adds depth to the family portrait. The mother's generous deposit shows her support for her children's passions.

🌍 Emigration (Imirce)

Dainín's Chicago connection is a reminder that emigration was woven into the fabric of life in the Gaeltacht. He went to America, worked, learned music, and returned — carrying that experience silently within him. The connection between Kerry and Chicago through music is quietly moving.

💛 Warmth and Positivity

Unlike many other chapters, Chapter 6 is almost entirely warm and positive. There is no punishment, no poverty-driven desperation, no mischief with consequences. It is a chapter about passion, discovery, family, and the joy that music brings — a welcome breath of pure warmth.
