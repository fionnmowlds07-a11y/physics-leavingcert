The Magdalene Sermon · 1989

Street

A man follows a butcher's daughter through a dark, dangerous street. Gothic mystery, powerful female presence, and an ambiguous ending that leaves everything unresolved.

Gothic Mystery Power of Women 2 Stanzas · Unrhymed Threatening Tone

The Simple Version

A man is watching a woman — "the butcher's daughter" — who is leaving work, still carrying a knife with blood on her clothing. He follows her through a dark, atmospheric street. She walks into her building and leaves bloodied footprints up the stairs. The poem ends ambiguously — we never know if he follows her or what happens next. The key thing: the woman is not the victim here. She is powerful, dangerous, and in control.

Background & Inspiration

🔪 Inspiration 1: The Butcher's Shop

Ní Chuilleanáin encountered a beautiful young woman in a butcher's shop — a surprising figure of beauty surrounded by blood and carcasses. This contrast between beauty and the grim context sparked the poem.

📖 Inspiration 2: Joseph Conrad's "The Rover"

The novel features Arlette, a mysterious, beautiful, disturbed young woman. Her aunt says "death clings to her skirts... let us keep her feet out of more human blood." From these two sources, Ní Ch. creates a Gothic mystery.

Central Themes

💪 Power of Women

Although the man is the one watching, the woman holds all the power. She carries a knife. She is surrounded by blood and shows no fear. It is the man who appears mesmerised and helpless — almost as if he is her prey. This reversal of the expected power dynamic is central to the poem.

⚡ Mystery & Ambiguity

Nothing is resolved. The woman's identity, her actions, and the man's fate are all left open. The street is unnamed, the characters unnamed. Even the blood is ambiguous — animal? Human? This deliberate mystery reflects Ní Ch.'s belief that poetry should allow the reader to find their own meaning.

🌹 Romance vs. Danger

The man is clearly drawn to this woman romantically — "infatuated." Yet the poem is full of threatening imagery: the knife, the blood, the dark shambles. Ní Ch. creates a tension between desire and danger throughout.

🎭 The Gothic

Gothic elements dominate: dark setting, mysterious woman, blood, half-open doors, shadows. The poem has the quality of a fairy tale where the hero pursues a dangerous creature. The woman is almost supernatural.

Stanza-by-Stanza Analysis

Stanza 1 — The Encounter
"He" follows the butcher's daughter, dangling a knife, in white with dark blood drops on the paving-stones...

"He" is unnamed and infatuated

The use of the pronoun "He" without a name keeps the man anonymous — just a watching figure. This also makes the poem universal: any man could be this figure. The woman dominates; the man is defined only by his act of following.

"Dangling a knife" — ease with the weapon

"Dangling" suggests carelessness, ease — she is completely at home with this weapon. This is her natural environment. The knife is a symbol of violence and power, yet she carries it as naturally as a bag. For the man watching, this combination of beauty and casual violence is irresistible and terrifying.

Is the blood animal or human?

Ní Ch. deliberately never clarifies. The ambiguity keeps the threatening quality alive — who or what has she butchered? "Dark shining drops" — the paradox of dark and shining creates a vivid, visceral image of blood catching the light on stone.

Stanza 2 — The Disappearance
"The shambles," the slanting lane, the half-open door, a flight of stairs, red crescent marks fading to faintest...

"The shambles" — her domain

A shambles is a rough marketplace where meat is carved and animals slaughtered — a place of violence and mayhem. To the outside world it is chaotic; to her, it is home. She is completely at ease in the place that would horrify anyone else.

"Half open door" — invitation and barrier

The half-open door is perfectly symbolic: an invitation we cannot fully accept. We can see in, but not fully. The man (and the reader) gets a glimpse, but the full picture is withheld.

"Red crescent marks... fading to faintest"

The blood on her feet forms crescent (half-moon) shapes as she climbs the stairs. "Crescent" is unexpectedly poetic — almost lunar, celestial. Combined with "fading to faintest," it creates the impression she is dissolving into another realm, becoming mythical. The poem ends with her traces disappearing — the ultimate mystery preserved.

Ní Chuilleanáin refuses to tell us what happens next. Does the man follow? Does he turn away? Is the woman dangerous, or is she simply going home? This ambiguity is deliberate. The reader is free to interpret. The woman's power is preserved: she remains mysterious, unknowable, mythical.

Poetic Techniques

👁️ Third Person Narrator

The detached "he" creates distance and tension. Characters are not described in detail — this adds to the universality and mystery. Cinematic quality: we observe but cannot intervene.

🎵 Sound Devices

Alliteration and consonance of t, p, b sounds mimics the sound of blood dropping and the heartbeat. Sibilance throughout: threatening hissing sounds ("shambles," "slanting," "stairs"). Musical "G" sounds add a playful quality.

⟶ Enjambment

Lines run into each other without stopping, showing movement and energy — particularly as the woman walks. Creates momentum of something in motion that cannot be stopped.

🎨 Colour Symbolism

White — traditional innocence, subverted here. Red — passion AND danger simultaneously. The duality is crucial: desire and threat are the same thing in this poem.

🔪 The Knife as Symbol

The knife represents violence, power, and threat. It is also a phallic symbol — by giving the woman the knife, Ní Ch. gives her the power society usually assigns to men. The man is symbolically powerless.

🌑 Gothic Imagery

"Dark shining drops / dangling a knife / slanting lane / red crescents" — threatening, dangerous, atmospheric images build a horror-story quality throughout. The setting is almost supernatural.

Context & Exam Preparation

🎓
Exam Tip

Always emphasise the reversal of power — this is the most important thing. The man appears to be the threat (he follows her) but she is the dangerous one. Discuss how Ní Ch. uses imagery, colour and setting to establish the woman's power. Always point to the ambiguous ending as a deliberate choice that preserves her mystery.

Links to Other Poems

  • The Bend in the Road: Both begin mid-action. Both have a mysterious, powerful atmosphere. Both use simple daily events elevated into something extraordinary.
  • Translation: Both feature powerful women. In Translation the women are victimised but given voice; in Street the woman holds power entirely.
  • Lucina: Both feature lone women in threatening, atmospheric settings who are ultimately powerful rather than victims.
💡
Key Argument for Exam

In "Street," Ní Chuilleanáin subverts expectations by making the observed woman the powerful figure and the observing man the vulnerable one. The blood, knife and dark setting that might appear to threaten the woman in fact originate from her — she is the source of danger, not its target.
