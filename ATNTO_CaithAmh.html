📋 Pastimes in the Autobiography

Caitheamh aimsire (pastimes) is one of the richest themes in the book. Because there was no electricity, no television, and very little money, the people of the Gaeltacht had to create their own entertainment — and they did so with remarkable energy and creativity. The pastimes in this book range from the organised (céilís, football championships) to the spontaneous (rough road football, illegal fishing) to the obsessive (cigarette collecting).

A key insight for the exam: many of Maidhc's pastimes also had a practical/economic dimension — fishing and selling the catch, playing accordion for £30, selling horse-tail hair. Pastimes and survival are often intertwined.

Every Pastime — Analysis + Phrases
⚽ Football — Chapters 1 & 7

Primary school: The boys played on the road using stones as goalposts, with no rules, no referee, and everyone keeping their own score. It was more like rugby than football — rough, chaotic, and joyful. It shows both the poverty of the time (no proper equipment) and the spirit of youth.

Vocational school: Football reaches its peak in Chapter 7 — organised competition, proper positions, a real coach (Micheál Ó hIarnáin), and ultimately the county championship win. Football runs as a thread through the whole autobiography, from rough road game to county glory.

✍️ Phrases to Use

Caitheamh aimsire a bhíodh acu gach aon lá ná cluiche peileTheir pastime every day was a game of football
Bhain siad úsáid as clocha mar chuaillíThey used stones as goalposts
Ní raibh rialacha nó réiteoir sa chluiche peileThere were no rules or referee in the football game
Bhí craobh an chontae buaite acu don chéad uair riamh — éacht iontachThey had won the county championship for the first time ever — a wonderful achievement
🐟 Fishing — Chapter 4

Illegal salmon fishing in the river on the border was both a pastime and an economic necessity. Maidhc and his father fished together — a bonding activity. The salmon were sold in Dingle for pocket money. The chapter contains the famous scene of Dainín falling in the river, showing that even the most serious activities can produce comedy.

✍️ Phrases to Use

Bhí ganntanas airgid ann agus bhí iascaireacht mhídhleathach go forleathanThere was a shortage of money and illegal fishing was widespread
Bhí scil speisialta ann chun píce iascaireachta a úsáidThere was a special skill to using a fishing pike
Bhí an-chuid suime ag Maidhc san iascaireachtMaidhc had a great interest in fishing
Is léir go raibh dea-chuimhní ag Maidhc ón gcaitheamh aimsire seoIt is clear that Maidhc has good memories from this pastime
🎵 Music — Chapters 6 & 8

Music is Maidhc's greatest and most enduring passion. It begins with a letter to Cox of Kilcock and the surprise of Dainín's hidden talent. By Chapter 8 the accordion earns Maidhc £30 a summer and a stage at the céilís from which he meets Eibhlín. Music is simultaneously pastime, income, and gateway to love.

✍️ Phrases to Use

Bhí an-suim ag Maidhc sa cheol, go háirithe sa bhosca ceoilMaidhc had a great interest in music, especially the accordion
Chaith Maidhc a am saor á sheinntMaidhc spent his free time playing it
Is léir go raibh an ceol rí-thábhachtach do Mhaidhc mar chaitheamh aimsireIt is clear that music was extremely important to Maidhc as a pastime
Bhí sé ag fáil £30 agus cead isteach saor do na céilithe móraHe was getting £30 and free admission to the big céilís
🏠 Bothántaíocht — Chapter 3

With no electricity or television, the main social activity was bothántaíocht — visiting neighbours in the evenings. The All-Ireland Final on the radio brought the whole parish to Maidhc's house. Grae the storyteller went from house to house sharing stories. This tradition was the social glue of the community.

✍️ Phrases to Use

Thagadh muintir an pharóiste chuig teach Maidhc chun éisteacht leis an raidióThe people of the parish used to come to Maidhc's house to listen to the radio
Bhíodh fear darbh ainm Grae ag dul ó theach go teach ag insint scéaltaA man called Grae used to go from house to house telling stories
Bhí orthu siamsaíocht a chruthú dóibh féinThey had to create entertainment for themselves
🚬 Cigarettes — Chapter 5

Smoking was the forbidden pastime — Maidhc was twelve, publicly smoking was considered grown-up at sixteen. The lengths he and Eddie went to — collecting butts, selling horse-tail hair, stealing eggs — show both poverty and the obsessive energy of youth. Though not presented as admirable, it is honestly and humorously told.

✍️ Phrases to Use

Fuair andúil ag Maidhc le toitíní mar thoradh ar a chairdeas le hEddieMaidhc got addicted to cigarettes as a result of his friendship with Eddie
Gach Luan rachadh siad go Halla an Pharóiste chun an tobac a bhailiúEvery Monday they would go to the Parish Hall to collect the tobacco
Léiríonn sé seo dearcadh na hóige — an fonn a bheith fásta suasThis shows the outlook of youth — the desire to be grown up

Model Essay Answer — Pastimes

📝 Full Model Paragraph Structure

Paragraph 1 — Football:
Chomh maith le cúrsaí scolaíochta, faighim léargas suimiúil den chaitheamh aimsire a bhí ag Maidhc. (As well as schooling matters, I get an interesting insight into the pastimes Maidhc had.) Caitheamh aimsire a bhíodh acu gach aon lá ná cluiche peile. (Their pastime every day was a game of football.) Bhain siad úsáid as clocha mar chuaillí agus ní raibh rialacha nó réiteoir sa chluiche. (They used stones as goalposts and there were no rules or referee in the game.) Is sampla iontach é den spraoi agus dearcadh na hóige. (It is a great example of the fun and outlook of youth.)

Paragraph 2 — Fishing:
Maidir leis an iascaireacht mar chaitheamh aimsire, bhí ganntanas airgid ann agus bhí iascaireacht mhídhleathach go forleathan. (Regarding fishing as a pastime, there was a shortage of money and illegal fishing was widespread.) Bhí Maidhc in ann airgead póca a dhéanamh as seo. (Maidhc was able to make pocket money from this.)

Paragraph 3 — Music:
Tá ról lárnach ag an gceol mar chaitheamh aimsire sa dírbheathaisnéis. (Music has a central role as a pastime in the autobiography.) Is léir go raibh an ceol rí-thábhachtach do Mhaidhc. (It is clear that music was extremely important to Maidhc.) Faoin am a bhí na cúrsaí Gaeltachta ar siúl, bhí sé ag seinm ag na céilithe agus ag fáil £30. (By the time the Gaeltacht courses were on, he was playing at the céilís and getting £30.)

Conclusion:
Mar fhocail scoir, is léir go raibh caitheamh aimsire ilghnéitheach ag Maidhc agus gur léirigh gach ceann acu dearcadh bríomhar na hóige. (To conclude, it is clear that Maidhc had a varied range of pastimes and each one showed the lively outlook of youth.)

💡
Key Strategy — Cover at Least Three Pastimes

In a pastimes essay, aim to cover at least three: football, fishing, and music are the strongest. If you can also mention bothántaíocht (community visiting), you show real breadth. Each pastime should have its own Irish quote and brief analysis — don't just list them, show what each one reveals about Maidhc's character and the community he lived in.
