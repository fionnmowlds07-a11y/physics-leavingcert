Téamaí & Mothúcháin

The themes, emotions and deeper meanings running through Mo Ghrá-sa. From the central theme of realistic love to the emotion of satisfaction — everything you need for exam questions on this poem.

Grá Réalaíoch Cineáltacht Áilleacht Inmheánach Sásamh Aoir
Príomhthéamaí — Main Themes
❤️ Téama 1 — Grá Réalaíoch (Realistic Love)

This is the central theme of the entire poem. Unlike the idealised, fantasy love depicted in the old song tradition, Ní Dhomhnaill presents a love that is messy, honest, imperfect — and all the more real for it.

"Tá sé ar intinn agam an téama sin a chíoradh agus a scagadh go cúramach." (I intend to dissect and examine this theme carefully.) She does not pretend her partner is handsome, or perfect, or like a figure from a romantic poem. She loves the actual man in front of her.

Tá a fhios ag an bhfile nach bhfuil a grá foirfe. Ní chuireann a lochtanna agus a laigí isteach ná amach uirthi.
The poet knows her love is not perfect. His faults and failings don't bother her at all.
→ From the notes: "mionrudaí iad" — they are small things, not worth worrying about.
Tá an caidreamh sin bunaithe ar chomhmheas agus chomhoibriú.
That relationship is based on mutual respect and cooperation.
→ The notes emphasise this as the foundation of the real relationship.

💙 Téama 2 — Cineáltacht & Tuiscint (Kindness & Understanding)

Kindness is presented as far more valuable than physical beauty. What does her partner actually do? He gives her apples — everyday necessities. And when he is in good form, he gives her grapes — small luxuries. These are not grand gestures. They are the texture of a caring relationship, day by day.

"Tá sé soléir go bhfuil an file an-bhuíoch as na gnáthrudaí a thugann sé di." (It is clear that the poet is very grateful for the normal things he gives her.) The warmth she feels is not born of passionate idealism but of quiet, genuine care.

Is duine flaithiúil, caoin agus cneasta é. Tugann sé riachtanais an tsaoil di agus tá siad an-cheanúil ar a chéile.
He is a generous, gentle and kind person. He gives her the necessities of life and they are very fond of each other.
→ From the teacher's notes — "sin sách maith di" (that is good enough for her).

💚 Téama 3 — Áilleacht Inmheánach vs. Áilleacht Sheachtrach

One of the poem's most modern and enduring messages is that inner beauty — personality, generosity, kindness — matters far more than outer appearance. The poet uses the structure of the traditional love poem (which obsessed over outer beauty) to make exactly the opposite point.

"Ceapaim go bhfuil an file ag aoradh dearcaidh na coitiantachta sa lá atá inniú, nuair a chuirtear an iomarca béime ar áilleacht sheachtrach agus déantar faillí ar áilleacht inmheánach." (I think the poet is satirising the common view today, when too much emphasis is placed on external beauty and inner beauty is neglected.)

Ní pictiúr ealaíonta é a leannán, ach tá a phearsantacht i bhfad Éireann níos tábhachtaí.
Her lover is not an oil painting, but his personality is far more important.
→ Teacher's notes: "pictiúr ealaíonta" (oil painting) — great phrase for the exam.

🟡 Téama 4 — An Aoir ar na Sean-Dánta Grá

The entire poem is structured as a satire of the 17th-century Irish love-song tradition. In those songs, women were routinely compared to flowers, swans, moonlight, and sunlight in their physical perfection. Ní Dhomhnaill takes this tradition apart, showing it to be unrealistic and even misleading about what love actually is.

D'fheadfá a rá gur dán frith-ghrá é! Ar lámh amháin tá an file ag magadh faoi na 'hamhráin grá' a bhí an-choitianta in Éireann sa seachtú haois déag.
One could say it is an anti-love poem! On one hand the poet is mocking the 'love songs' that were very common in Ireland in the 17th century.
→ From the teacher's notes — excellent framing for a satire question.

She also connects implicitly to Shakespeare's Sonnet 130, which performs the same satirical move in English — both poets agreeing that fake, flowery praise does a disservice to real human love.

💜 Téama 5 — An Grá Dall (Love is Blind)

The concept that "love is blind" — tá an grá dall — runs through the whole poem. She sees every one of his flaws perfectly clearly and describes them with comic precision. And yet she loves him entirely. The ability to see someone's imperfections and love them anyway is presented as a mature, realistic, and ultimately beautiful form of love.

Cé nach bhfuil sé dathúil sa bhealach traidisiúnta, tá an grá dall. D'aithin sí an seanfhocal "ní bhíonn saoi gan locht."
Even though he is not good-looking in the traditional way, love is blind. She recognises the proverb "nobody is perfect."
→ Use "ní bhíonn saoi gan locht" as a closing phrase — it's a proverb that exam markers love.

Mothúcháin — Emotions in the Poem

❤️ Príomhmhothúcháin

The dominant emotion in this poem shifts dramatically. It opens in a tone of comic mockery and seemingly cold observation, but the poem's emotional journey ends somewhere much warmer. Here are the key emotions to discuss:

😄 Greann — Humour

The dominant emotion for much of the poem. The comic comparisons (barbed wire hair, daisies from ears) create a tone of affectionate mockery. This is not cruel — it is the kind of teasing that exists in genuinely loving relationships.

😊 Sásamh — Satisfaction

"I mo thuairim, is é sásamh an mothúchán is láidre sa dán greannmhar seo." The poet is deeply content with her relationship. Despite everything she has noted, she is happy. This satisfaction is the poem's emotional conclusion.

🙏 Buíochas — Gratitude

She is grateful for the simple things he gives her — the apples, the grapes. This ordinary gratitude for ordinary gifts is depicted as the foundation of a real, lasting relationship.

💛 Cion — Affection/Fondness

"Tá siad an-cheanúil ar a chéile." (They are very fond of each other.) Beneath all the mockery is a warm, quiet fondness — the everyday love of two people who genuinely like each other.

🎭 An Athrú Mothúcháin — The Emotional Shift

One of the most powerful structural features of this poem is the dramatic emotional shift at "Ach is cuma sin." Everything before this line is comic, even cutting. Everything after it is tender.

Ag tús an dáin, cuireann sí i gcéill go bhfuil sí míshásta lena grá. Ach ar deireadh thiar thall, tagaimid ar an bhfírinne.
At the start of the poem, she pretends she is unhappy with her love. But eventually, we arrive at the truth.
→ From the teacher's notes — use "ar deireadh thiar thall" for "eventually/finally."
Is cuma léi nach bhfuil a leannán tarraingteach. Ag deireadh an lae, tá an-chion aige uirthi agus tá sí an-tógtha leis freisin.
She doesn't care that her lover is not attractive. At the end of the day, he is very fond of her and she is very taken with him too.
→ "An-tógtha leis" — very taken with him — excellent phrase.

An Teachtaireacht — The Message

📢 Cad é teachtaireacht an dáin?

Ní Dhomhnaill has several connected messages running through the poem. Together they form a philosophy of love that is honest, mature, and quietly radical.

🎓
The 5 Messages — Learn These
  • Love is not what the old songs say it is. The traditional Irish love-song created an impossible, unrealistic ideal. Real love is something else entirely.
  • Inner beauty is more important than outer beauty. Áilleacht inmheánach > áilleacht sheachtrach.
  • Everyone has flaws — and that's fine. Ní bhíonn saoi gan locht. Accepting someone's imperfections is part of loving them.
  • Small acts of kindness are the foundation of love. Apples and grapes, given consistently and freely, matter more than grand romantic gestures.
  • Love is blind. Tá an grá dall. Seeing someone's faults clearly — and choosing to love them anyway — is the most honest form of love.
📢 Cén fáth go bhfuil an teachtaireacht seo tábhachtach?

Ní Dhomhnaill wrote this poem in the 20th century but its message feels more relevant than ever in an age of social media perfection and impossible beauty standards. She is arguing — with wit, humour, and genuine warmth — that the love worth having is not the love in the songs, but the love in the kitchen: simple, imperfect, kind, and real.

Ceapaim go bhfuil an file ag aoradh dearcaidh na coitiantachta sa lá atá inniu, nuair a chuirtear an iomarca béime ar áilleacht sheachtrach agus déantar faillí ar áilleacht inmheánach.
I think the poet is satirising the common view today, when too much emphasis is placed on external beauty and inner beauty is neglected.
→ From the teacher's notes — a great point about contemporary relevance.

Freagra Samplach — Model Answer
🎓 Question: Discuss the main theme and what you like about this poem.
Aontaím go huile is go hiomlán leis an raiteas thuasluaite. Is cinnte gur dán grámhar é seo, cé nach gceapfá é sin ar an gcéad léamh. Is léir go bhfuil cur síos scigmhagúil agus aibhéalach ann ó thús deireadh a chabhraíonn leis an téama sin a léiriú — tá an grá dall.
I totally agree with the above statement. It is certain that this is a love poem, even though you would not think that on first reading. It is clear that there is a derisive and exaggerated description throughout that helps portray that theme — love is blind.
Forbraítear an dán agus leanann an file ar aghaidh go scigmhagúil. Sonraíonn sí na lochtanna ar a aghaidh, ar a shúile agus ar a chuid gruaige — "tá a shúile ró-chóngarach dá chéile" agus "mar a bheadh sreang dheilgneach ann." Ag an bpointe seo, éiríonn go géal leis an bhfile fiosracht an léitheora a mhúscailt.
The poem develops and the poet continues in a mocking tone. She notes the faults on his face, his eyes and his hair — "his eyes are too close together" and "like barbed wire." At this point, the poet successfully evokes the curiosity of the reader.
Sa véarsa deireanach, athraíonn gach rud. "Ach is cuma sin" — is cuma léi a lochtanna. Tugann sé di úlla agus caora finiúna. Is meafar iad seo don ghrá agus don chineáltacht. Dar léi, tá pearsantacht níos tábhachtaí ná cuma.
In the last verse, everything changes. "But none of that matters" — his faults don't matter to her. He gives her apples and grapes. These are a metaphor for love and kindness. According to her, personality is more important than appearance.
Mar fhocail scoir, ní féidir a shéanadh gurb é príomhthéama an dáin seo ná an tábhacht a bhaineann le cineáltacht. D'aithin sí an seanfhocal "ní bhíonn saoi gan locht." Sin an fhírinne atá ag croí an dáin seo.
Finally, there is no denying that the main theme of this poem is the importance of kindness. She recognises the proverb "nobody is perfect." That is the truth at the heart of this poem.
