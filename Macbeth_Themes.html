2026 — Mock Paper 2, Option A
(i) "In his play, Macbeth, William Shakespeare presents a world where guilt cannot be suppressed." Discuss this statement.
Focus: Guilt in Macbeth (dagger, murder sleep, scorpions), Lady Macbeth (a little water → damned spot), Banquo's ghost. Argue guilt operates differently at each stage but can never be eliminated — only delayed until it becomes madness.
2026 — Mock Paper 2, Option A
(ii) Discuss how Shakespeare uses characters to explore the consequences of both flawed and virtuous leadership.
Focus: Duncan (virtuous — plant thee, sainted king), Macbeth (flawed — tyranny, scorpions, tomorrow), Malcolm (restoration). The question is asking for both sides — spend equal time on each. Use Banquo and Macduff as additional virtuous foils.
2026 — Mock Paper 2, Option B
(i) How do Macbeth's actions and behaviour reveal his internal conflict with ambition and guilt in his pursuit of power?
Focus: The tension between ambition and conscience. Show the arc: vaulting ambition (1.7), murder sleep (2.2), scorpions (3.2), stepped in blood (3.4), tomorrow (5.5). Each quote shows a different stage of this conflict resolving in ambition's favour — until ambition burns everything away.
2026 — Mock Paper 2, Option B
(ii) How does Shakespeare use language and imagery to intensify the portrayal of Scotland under Macbeth's rule?
Focus: Blood imagery (stepped in blood), sleep imagery (murder sleep), clothing imagery ("borrowed robes"), darkness/light (stars hide your fires). Also: what other characters say about Macbeth's Scotland — "new widows howl, new orphans cry" (Macduff, Act 4.3). Great opportunity to mention trochaic tetrameter of witches = disorder in language itself.

2023 — Leaving Cert Paper 2
(i) "Macbeth's unstable and tragic identity is shaped by a variety of ambiguities and complexities in his character." Discuss this statement.
Focus: Show the five stages of Macbeth's arc. "Unstable" = show how drastically he changes. "Tragic" = he is aware of his own moral failure (vaulting ambition, virtues will plead like angels). "Complexities" = he is simultaneously the most evil and most self-aware character in the play. Use the arc tab on Macbeth's page.
2023 — Leaving Cert Paper 2
(ii) "Shakespeare makes effective use of Lady Macbeth and the Witches to heighten the dramatic impact of his play Macbeth in a variety of ways."
Focus: Lady Macbeth: dramatic impact through the reversal (strongest → first destroyed). Witches: dramatic impact through equivocation, paradox, and the second meeting (Act 4). Compare their methods — one works by prophecy/equivocation; one by direct psychological manipulation. Both are catalysts; neither fully controls Macbeth.
2023 — Deferred Exam
(i) Identify what you believe to be the three most important choices or decisions Macbeth makes. Discuss why these choices are important.
Focus: Suggested choices: (1) Deciding to murder Duncan despite his own reasoning (Act 1.7 — vaulting ambition). (2) Ordering Banquo's murder (Act 3 — scorpions — the step from reactive to proactive evil). (3) Visiting the witches again in Act 4 (choosing to deepen his involvement with dark powers rather than retreat). Each choice shows a different stage of deterioration.
2023 — Deferred Exam
(ii) Identify aspects of the play that lead us to draw disturbing conclusions about evil.
Focus: The most disturbing aspect: Macbeth is fully aware of his own evil (vaulting ambition, virtues will plead like angels) and acts anyway. Evil requires a willing participant, not an unwilling victim. Lady Macbeth inviting dark spirits — she has to work to become evil. The witches never commanding — evil emerges from within, not from without.
2023 — Mock Paper A
(i) "Shakespeare uses a variety of techniques to create a world of deception in Macbeth."
Focus: This is an Appearance vs Reality question framed as technique. Name the techniques: dramatic irony (Duncan at Inverness), equivocation (witches — none of woman born), aside (stars hide your fires), soliloquy (private truth vs public performance). The serpent/flower quote from Lady Macbeth is your anchor.
2023 — Mock Paper A
(ii) "Both Macbeth and Lady Macbeth display a variety of character traits and values that contribute to the dramatic and tragic outcome."
Focus: Compare their traits: Macbeth — moral awareness, guilt, deterioration into nihilism. Lady Macbeth — decisiveness, suppression of conscience, early collapse. The "dramatic and tragic" framing means you should discuss both what they do (dramatic) and what they lose (tragic). Their reversed dynamic is the heart of this answer.
2023 — Mock Paper B
(i) "The play Macbeth presents us with an entirely pessimistic view of the theme of justice."
Focus: Agree or disagree — the best approach is nuanced: partly pessimistic (Duncan is murdered; Banquo is murdered; Lady Macduff and her children are slaughtered). But justice is restored in Act 5: Macbeth is killed; Malcolm is crowned; natural order is restored. The play is pessimistic about justice in the short term but optimistic about its eventual restoration.
2023 — Mock Paper B
(ii) "Both Banquo and Macduff fulfil important dramatic functions in the play Macbeth."
Focus: Banquo's function: moral foil (proves free will), his ghost (externalises guilt), his descendants (the restored order). Macduff's function: loyal subject, avenger, the man who kills Macbeth, the voice of Scotland's suffering ("new widows howl"). Together they represent Scotland's integrity — everything Macbeth tried to destroy.

2019 — Leaving Cert Paper 2
(i) Discuss the reasons why Shakespeare's use of horrific, bizarre and unbelievable elements does or does not heighten the tragic intensity.
Focus: The horrific elements — witches, ghost, sleepwalking, cauldron, hallucinated dagger — all serve psychological purposes. They externalise inner states: the dagger is guilt before the act; the ghost is guilt after. The sleepwalking is guilt breaking through. The horrific is not gratuitous; it is the language of the unconscious made visible.
2019 — Mock Paper 2
(i) "The values evident in the play, Macbeth, have a profound influence on the tragic outcomes."
Focus: Values = honour, loyalty, ambition, guilt, justice. Show how each value drives outcomes: Macbeth's ambition drives the murders; his honour makes him feel guilty; his guilt destroys him. Lady Macbeth's suppression of guilt fails. Banquo's honour keeps him loyal. Duncan's trust makes him vulnerable. Each character embodies a value that leads directly to their outcome.
2014 — Leaving Cert
(i) "Macbeth's relationships with other characters can be seen primarily as power struggles which prove crucial to the outcome."
Focus: Macbeth vs Duncan (king vs subject, usurped), Macbeth vs Lady Macbeth (control shifts from her to him), Macbeth vs Banquo (rival for legitimacy), Macbeth vs Macduff (nemesis, eventual killer). Each relationship is a power dynamic that shifts — and the shifts drive the plot.
2014 — Mock Paper 2
(i) "Lady Macbeth's strengths and weaknesses lead to her downfall."
Focus: Her strength: decisive, controlled, manipulative (unsex me, screw your courage). Her weakness: she suppresses rather than resolves guilt (a little water). Her downfall: Act 5 sleepwalking — the suppressed guilt returns amplified. The argument: her strengths are the mechanism of her weakness. Her decisiveness prevents her from processing what she has done.
2013 — Leaving Cert
(i) "Macbeth, despite the influence of the witches and Lady Macbeth, is responsible for his own downfall."
Focus: The strongest possible Leaving Cert answer. Three-point structure: (1) Banquo receives same prophecy and resists — proves free will. (2) Macbeth's own Act 1.7 soliloquy — he reasons himself to the right answer then reverses. (3) Stepped in blood (Act 3.4) — he explicitly chooses to continue when he could stop. Conclusion: the witches and Lady Macbeth are catalysts; the choices are his.
2007 — Leaving Cert
(i) "The relationship between Macbeth and Lady Macbeth undergoes significant change during the course of the play."
Focus: Three stages: Act 1 (she leads — screw your courage, look like the innocent flower), Act 3 (he acts without her — "be innocent of the knowledge"), Act 5 (she collapses; he receives news with nihilism). Show the complete power reversal. Quote their language to each other at each stage to show how the tone changes.
Theme: Ambition

🔥 The Central Argument

Macbeth is a play about unchecked ambition. But Shakespeare is making a specific, sophisticated argument: ambition is not simply bad — it is self-defeating. Macbeth desires the crown and destroys everything that would make wearing it worthwhile. By the time he achieves his ambition, he has lost his wife, his peace of mind, his allies, his honour, and ultimately his sense that life means anything at all.

The key distinction: ambition guided by honour (Banquo has ambition for his descendants but never acts dishonourably) vs ambition freed from all moral constraint (Macbeth). The play argues the second type destroys the person who pursues it.

Cross-Character Quotes for Ambition
Macbeth
"I have no spur... but only / Vaulting ambition."
Act 1.7
He names his own flaw. The metaphor predicts his arc — reach too high, fall hard. He knows this and acts anyway. That is what makes him tragic.
Macbeth
"Stars, hide your fires; / Let not light see my black and deep desires."
Act 1.4
His ambition pre-dates the witches. These are his desires — possessive. The witches don't create his ambition; they name it.
Lady M.
"Nought's had, all's spent, / Where our desire is got without content."
Act 3.2
They have achieved their ambition and feel nothing from it. Achieved desire without satisfaction. This is Shakespeare's verdict on unchecked ambition: it cannot satisfy.
Macbeth
"Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow, / Creeps in this petty pace from day to day."
Act 5.5
The ultimate cost of ambition: he has gained everything and found life meaningless. Nihilism is the end point of unchecked desire.
Banquo
"Lesser than Macbeth, and greater. / Not so happy, yet much happier."
Act 1.3
The witches' paradox turns out to be true: Banquo never sought the crown and dies with more peace than Macbeth has as king. Choosing honour over ambition leads to the greater life.
💡
Ambition Essay Argument

Structure your answer around three stages: (1) Ambition desired — what Macbeth wants and why (stars, hide your fires; vaulting ambition). (2) Ambition achieved — immediate cost (scorpions, murder sleep). (3) Ambition's endpoint — nihilism (tomorrow). Then use Banquo as contrast — same prophecy, no unchecked action, dies with honour intact. The comparison proves the argument.

Theme: Guilt

😰 The Central Argument

Guilt in Macbeth is not a passing emotion — it is a progressive, inescapable force that operates differently at different stages but always deepens. Shakespeare shows that guilt cannot be suppressed: Lady Macbeth tries hardest to suppress it and is the first destroyed by it. The play argues that a conscience, once violated, cannot be restored — only drowned deeper, until it surfaces as madness.

Cross-Character Quotes for Guilt
Macbeth
"Methought I heard a voice cry, 'Sleep no more! / Macbeth does murder sleep.'"
Act 2.2
Guilt arrives immediately — before anyone suspects him, before any consequences. The hallucinated voice is his own conscience. Guilt is internal and instantaneous.
Lady M.
"A little water clears us of this deed: / How easy is it then!"
Act 2.2
Dramatic irony — she is wrong. This line sets up the most powerful contrast in the play: her Act 2 dismissal of guilt vs her Act 5 inability to wash it away. Water does not clear the deed.
Macbeth
"Thou canst not say I did it: never shake / Thy gory locks at me."
Act 3.4
Banquo's ghost is guilt made visible. Macbeth's denial ("thou canst not say I did it") is addressed to a ghost only he can see — the private guilt breaking into the public space.
Lady M.
"Out, damned spot! out, I say!"
Act 5.1
The guilt she suppressed in Act 2 returns amplified, inescapable, sensory. She can see and smell blood that isn't there. The sleepwalking is guilt breaking through the sleep she denied Macbeth.
Lady M.
"All the perfumes of Arabia will not sweeten this little hand."
Act 5.1
The scale of guilt has expanded from "a little water" to "all the perfumes of Arabia." Guilt does not diminish — it grows. Nothing in the world is large enough to cancel what she has done.
💡
Guilt Essay Argument — The Best Structure

Show guilt in three stages: (1) Immediate — Macbeth's hallucinations immediately after the murder (murder sleep, dagger). (2) Suppressed — Lady Macbeth's "a little water" — she denies it; it goes underground. (3) Returned and amplified — both are destroyed: Banquo's ghost (Macbeth), sleepwalking (Lady Macbeth). The argument: guilt cannot be suppressed — suppression only makes the eventual reckoning more devastating.

Theme: Leadership & Kingship

👑 The Central Argument

Macbeth is a play about what good and bad leadership look like. Shakespeare presents two models: Duncan — generous, trusting, loved — and Macbeth — paranoid, murderous, feared. The play argues strongly that legitimate authority comes from virtue, not power. Macbeth has the crown but not the legitimacy. His reign destroys Scotland; Duncan's death is mourned by everyone including Macbeth himself.

Malcolm's speech listing "king-becoming graces" (justice, verity, temperance, stableness) is the play's explicit statement of what good leadership requires — and it is the qualities Macbeth lacks entirely.

Cross-Character Quotes for Leadership
Duncan
"I have begun to plant thee, and will labour / To make thee full of growing."
Act 1.4
The ideal king as gardener — nurturing, investing in subjects, thinking of the future. Duncan's leadership is generative, not self-serving.
Duncan
"There's no art / To find the mind's construction in the face."
Act 1.4
His fatal vulnerability: he cannot see through appearances. Trust is his virtue and his downfall. Shakespeare shows that virtuous leadership can be exploited by the unvirtuous.
Macbeth
"O, full of scorpions is my mind, dear wife!"
Act 3.2
The mind of a tyrant: not justice or temperance but paranoia and fear. A king who rules by fear rather than respect has already failed at leadership.
Macbeth
"Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow / Creeps in this petty pace from day to day."
Act 5.5
A king who finds life meaningless has completely abandoned his duty to Scotland. Leadership requires purpose; Macbeth has none. This is the complete failure of leadership expressed as nihilism.
Duncan
"...the most sainted king."
Act 4.3 (Malcolm about Duncan)
In death, Duncan has become the model against which Macbeth's tyranny is measured. His memory is the standard Scotland will return to through Malcolm's restoration.
💡
Leadership Essay Argument

Structure: (1) Duncan as the ideal — plant thee, sainted king, trust. (2) Macbeth as its opposite — scorpions, tyranny, nihilism. (3) The consequences for Scotland — Macduff: "new widows howl, new orphans cry." (4) Malcolm's restoration as the return of Duncanesque values. The play argues that kingship divorced from virtue destroys both king and kingdom.

Theme: Appearance vs Reality / Deception

🎭 The Central Argument

"Fair is foul, and foul is fair" — this paradox is the play's thesis statement, delivered in its very first line. Appearance vs Reality is not just a theme in Macbeth; it is the structural principle of the entire play. Almost every scene contains a gap between how things appear and what they really are: the gracious king is about to be murdered by his loyal subject; the beautiful castle is a death-trap; the brave warrior is plotting regicide; the confident queen is disintegrating internally.

Cross-Character Quotes for Appearance vs Reality
Witches
"Fair is foul, and foul is fair."
Act 1.1
The play's thesis, stated before any human character speaks. Everything that follows is an exploration of this inversion. Macbeth himself echoes it ("so foul and fair a day") without realising he's speaking the witches' language.
Lady M.
"Look like the innocent flower, / But be the serpent under't."
Act 1.5
The theme stated as direct instruction. Lady Macbeth teaches Macbeth to perform appearance while hiding reality. She is the play's greatest practitioner of this — until Act 5, when the practice destroys her.
Duncan
"There's no art / To find the mind's construction in the face."
Act 1.4
Duncan's confession that he cannot read reality from appearance. He is the primary victim of the gap between face and mind. His trust is his goodness — and his fatal vulnerability.
Witches
"None of woman born / Shall harm Macbeth."
Act 4.1
The witches' equivocation — technically true, fatally misleading. Macbeth hears invincibility; the witches know about Macduff. This is the most deadly gap between apparent and real meaning in the play.
Lady M.
"Out, damned spot! out, I say!"
Act 5.1
In the sleepwalking scene, the gap collapses. Lady Macbeth's performed composure — maintained through Acts 1–4 — is shattered. Reality breaks through her appearance in the one state she cannot control: sleep. The doctor sees what she hid. Appearance and reality merge in destruction.
💡
Appearance vs Reality Essay Argument

Three layers: (1) The witches create it — equivocation means truth conceals falsehood. (2) The characters practise it — Lady Macbeth coaches Macbeth to deceive; both maintain public composure while privately disintegrating. (3) It destroys them — Lady Macbeth's sleepwalking is the gap collapsing; Macbeth's ghost-seeing at the banquet is the gap collapsing. The play argues you cannot maintain the gap between appearance and reality indefinitely — it always collapses.

Theme: The Supernatural

🧙 The Central Argument

The supernatural in Macbeth serves three functions: (1) It creates atmosphere — thunder, darkness, witches, ghosts; (2) It externalises internal states — the dagger, Banquo's ghost, and Lady Macbeth's sleepwalking are hallucinations of guilty conscience made visible; (3) It raises the free will vs fate question — if the witches can predict the future, is Macbeth fated? The answer the play gives: no. Banquo proves this.

Key Supernatural Quotes
Witches
"Fair is foul, and foul is fair."
Act 1.1
The supernatural announces itself through moral inversion. The witches don't just predict evil — they embody the inversion of natural order that Macbeth's reign will produce.
Banquo
"The instruments of darkness tell us truths, / Win us with honest trifles, to betray's / In deepest consequence."
Act 1.3
The most important analysis of the witches' method in the play — spoken by the man who resists them. The supernatural works through partial truths, not outright lies. This is their danger.
Lady M.
"Come, you spirits / That tend on mortal thoughts, unsex me here."
Act 1.5
Lady Macbeth invites the supernatural into herself. The parallel with the witches is deliberate: both she and the witches call on dark forces; she wants to become what they are.
Macbeth
"Methought I heard a voice cry, 'Sleep no more! / Macbeth does murder sleep.'"
Act 2.2
The hallucinated voice is the supernatural as conscience. Whether real or imagined, its effect is identical — it tells Macbeth what he already knows. The supernatural externalises his own moral awareness.
💡
The Examiner's Favourite Point About the Supernatural

Ask whether the supernatural events are "real" or psychological. The dagger, the voice, and Banquo's ghost may all be hallucinations — products of Macbeth's guilty imagination. Lady Macbeth's sleepwalking is definitely psychological. It doesn't matter whether they are "real" — their function is the same either way: they externalise guilt. This is a sophisticated point that shows you're engaging with the text analytically, not just retelling it.

How to Write a High-Scoring Macbeth Essay
⚠️
The Most Common Mistake

Retelling the plot instead of analysing the text. Every sentence in your essay should be analytical — you should be answering "how does Shakespeare show this?" not "what happens next?" If you find yourself writing "then Macbeth kills Duncan," stop and ask: what technique is Shakespeare using here, and what does it show?

The Paragraph Formula — PEE+
P

Point — Your Argument

State your analytical claim clearly. Don't start with a quote — start with what you're going to prove. Example: "Shakespeare presents Macbeth as a character who makes his choices with full moral awareness, making his tragedy entirely self-chosen."

E

Evidence — Quote + Technique

Give a short, accurate quote and name the technique. Example: "In his Act 1.7 soliloquy, Macbeth uses an extended equestrian metaphor — 'vaulting ambition, which o'erleaps itself / And falls on th' other' — to diagnose his own fatal flaw."

Short quotes are better than long ones. Two or three carefully chosen words are better than half a speech misquoted.

E

Explanation — Effect on Audience + Link to Theme

This is the most important part. Explain what the quote does — how it makes the audience feel, what it reveals about character, how it develops the theme. Example: "The metaphor of the rider who over-vaults and falls is prophetic of his entire arc. Shakespeare creates an unsettling dramatic irony: Macbeth sees his own downfall clearly, names it, and then ignores it. This develops the theme of ambition as self-defeating — the very intelligence that might save him becomes the mechanism of his fall."

+

Extension — Link to Another Character or Scene

Connect to a contrasting or parallel moment. Example: "This self-awareness distinguishes Macbeth from Lady Macbeth, who invokes supernatural forces to suppress her conscience rather than reason with it — suggesting Shakespeare sees Macbeth as the more complex tragic figure."

Introduction Formula

✍️ How to Open Your Essay

Three sentences: (1) Name the text, author, and central argument of the play. (2) State your response to the question directly — agree, disagree, or nuance. (3) Briefly signal your structure (which three or four aspects you'll discuss).

Example: "In William Shakespeare's tragedy Macbeth, the central argument is that unchecked ambition is self-defeating — it destroys not only those around the ambitious person but the person themselves. The statement that 'guilt cannot be suppressed' in the play is entirely accurate: both Macbeth and Lady Macbeth are destroyed by guilt, but by different mechanisms and at different speeds. In this essay, I will examine how Shakespeare shows guilt operating through hallucination, paranoia, and finally madness to demonstrate that a violated conscience cannot be permanently suppressed."

Key Techniques to Name and Use

Soliloquy

When a character speaks alone on stage. It reveals their true thoughts — the gap between public and private self. Macbeth's soliloquies show his moral awareness; Lady Macbeth's show her hidden collapse.

"I have no spur... but only / Vaulting ambition." (1.7)

Dramatic Irony

When the audience knows something a character doesn't. Duncan praises Inverness not knowing it's a death-trap. Lady Macbeth says "a little water" not knowing she'll spend Act 5 washing hands obsessively.

"This castle hath a pleasant seat." (1.6)

Equivocation

Using technically true statements to deceive. The witches' specialty. "None of woman born shall harm Macbeth" — true, but not the truth Macbeth thinks he's hearing.

"None of woman born / Shall harm Macbeth." (4.1)

Blood Imagery

Blood appears throughout as a symbol of guilt, crime, and consequence. It is both literal (on Macbeth's hands) and symbolic (the guilt that stains his soul and cannot be washed away).

"I am in blood / Stepped in so far..." (3.4)

Sleep Symbolism

Sleep represents innocence and peace. Macbeth "murders sleep" — and both he and Lady Macbeth lose all peace. Duncan is murdered sleeping; Lady Macbeth's guilt erupts in sleep. Sleep connects Duncan's murder to both murderers' punishment.

"Macbeth does murder sleep." (2.2)

Light / Dark Imagery

Light = goodness, truth, natural order. Dark = evil, concealment, ambition. Macbeth and Lady Macbeth both invoke darkness to hide their plans. The witches operate in fog and filthy air.

"Stars, hide your fires." (1.4)

Antithesis / Paradox

Stating opposites together. The play is built on paradoxes: fair/foul, lost/won, lesser/greater. Shakespeare uses antithesis to show how moral inversions operate throughout the play.

"Fair is foul, and foul is fair." (1.1)

Repetition / Anaphora

Repeating a word or structure for emphasis. "Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow" — the triple repetition enacts the monotonous, hopeless passage of time Macbeth is describing.

"Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow." (5.5)
💡
The Golden Rule for Every Paragraph

Technique → Quote → Effect → Theme. Name the technique Shakespeare uses. Give a short accurate quote. Explain what effect this creates for the audience. Link to the theme the question is asking about. Do this consistently for every paragraph and you will score very well regardless of which question you choose.

🔗
Conclusion Formula

Three sentences: (1) Restate your answer to the question directly — don't introduce new points. (2) Summarise what Shakespeare has achieved overall. (3) Give your own genuine response to the play — why it still matters, what it reveals about human nature. Examiners reward students who engage personally with the text.
